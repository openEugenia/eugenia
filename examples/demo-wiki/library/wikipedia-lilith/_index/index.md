The article traces the evolution of the figure of Lilith from her origins in Mesopotamian mythology to her role in medieval Jewish traditions. Lilith is introduced as a demonic entity linked to the wind and storms in early Mesopotamian texts, later associated with the night and sexual temptation. In Jewish tradition, she is depicted as Adam’s first wife, created alongside him from the earth, who refused to submit to him and left the Garden of Eden. Medieval Jewish texts, such as the *Alphabet of Ben Sira*, elaborate on her demonic nature, her connection to child mortality, and her role as a seductress of men. The article also explores her symbolic transformation in folklore and Kabbalah, where she becomes a figure of both danger and independence.
